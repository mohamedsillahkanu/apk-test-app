Cut & sew an ankara short-sleeve top

The everyday African print top with a round neck and drop shoulders — four pattern pieces, straight seams, no zip. Put your own measurements in and every cutting number on this page changes with them.

01 The style

Loose through the body, short sleeves cut in one straight line from the shoulder, a faced round neck and a plain hem. It suits a 6-yard ankara piece, a wax print, or kente-print cotton. Two yards makes an adult top.

05 Cutting the cloth

Ankara has a printed right side and a plain wrong side. Fold with the right sides facing each other so your chalk marks stay on the inside.

Layout on 115 cm cloth, folded down the middle

—

  1. Straighten the cloth first

    Pull a thread across the cut end and cut along that line, or tear across the grain. A crooked start twists the whole top.

  2. Fold and pin

    Fold the cloth along its length, right sides together, selvedges level. Pin the two layers together every hand-span so nothing shifts.

    Check the print direction now. If the pattern has flowers or figures that stand up, both panels must run the same way up.

  7. Cut, and mark before you unpin

    Cut with long strokes, keeping the scissors on the table. Before unpinning, snip a small notch at the middle of each sleeve top edge and at the shoulder line of the panels — those notches are how you line the pieces up at the machine.

    Cut the neck facing from the leftover cloth using the neckline you have just cut as the guide, 5 cm wide all round.

06 Sewing it together

Seams are 1.5 cm. Press every seam as you finish it — pressing is what separates a home-made look from a tailored one.

  1. Join the shoulders

    Right sides together, stitch the front to the back along both shoulder lines. Neaten the raw edges with a zigzag or an overlocker, then press them towards the back.

  2. Face the neck

    Join the facing at its shoulders too, and press. Pin it to the neck edge, right sides together, matching the shoulder seams. Stitch all round 1 cm from the edge, then trim the seam back to 6 mm and clip small V-notches around the curve so it will lie flat.

    Understitch: turn the facing out and stitch it to the seam allowance close to the seam. This stops the facing rolling to the outside when it is worn.

  3. Set in the sleeves flat

    With the top still open at the sides, lay a sleeve on the armhole, right sides together, matching the notch at the sleeve top to the shoulder seam. Stitch, neaten and press towards the sleeve. Do the second sleeve the same way.

    Sewing the sleeve in while the side seams are still open is far easier than setting it into a closed armhole, and it is how a drop-shoulder top is meant to be made.

  4. Close each side in one seam

    Fold the top right sides together. Starting at the cuff, stitch down the sleeve underarm and straight on down the side of the body to the hem, in one line. Do the same on the other side.

    Match the underarm seams exactly where they cross. A step there shows every time the wearer lifts an arm.

  5. Hem the sleeves and the bottom

    Turn 1 cm to the wrong side, press, turn another 1.5 cm, press again, then stitch close to the fold. Work the sleeve hems on the machine's smallest area, or by hand if the opening is tight.

  6. Press, and check the fit

    Press the finished top: shoulders first, then the sleeves, then the body, then the hem. Try it on and note anything to change — write it on your paper pattern so the next one is better.

    Keep the paper pieces with the measurements written on them. That is how a tailor builds a set of blocks that fit real customers.

Before cutting good ankara, make this once in cheap cotton or an old wrapper. A test garment tells you in an hour what a ruined 6-yard piece teaches the hard way.
